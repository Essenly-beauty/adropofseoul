Old Seoul is not one straight sightseeing street. Gyeongbokgung, Seochon and Tongin sit west of the main palace axis; Insadong, Ikseon-dong and Bukchon sit east. The old version of this guide crossed that axis twice. This one does not.

## Quick answer

Allow a full day. Start at **Gyeongbokgung Station**, finish near **Jongno 3-ga**, and wear shoes for stone courtyards. Check the palace's official calendar before travel. A hanbok—not a hanok—is traditional Korean dress.

| At a glance | Our recommendation |
|---|---|
| Best for | First-time visitors, history, crafts and traditional architecture |
| Time needed | 7–9 hours |
| Start / finish | Gyeongbokgung Station / Jongno 3-ga Station |
| Walking | About 6–8 km, with stone surfaces and optional Bukchon hills |
| Best day | Any palace opening day; avoid building the route around a closure |
| Key constraint | Bukchon red-zone tourist access is limited to 10am–5pm |

## Morning: Gyeongbokgung

Give the palace time before the day becomes a food-and-shopping walk. Guard ceremonies and opening hours can change because of weather or official events, so treat them as a bonus rather than the spine of the plan.

## Lunch: Seochon and Tongin Market

Leave through the palace's western side and continue into Seochon. Tongin Market is west of Gyeongbokgung, which is why it belongs here—not after Ikseon-dong. Browse first and decide whether a market lunch or a small neighborhood restaurant fits your appetite. Individual market programs and vendor days can change.

## Afternoon choice: Bukchon or Insadong

This is where the day should branch.

Choose **Bukchon** if preserved residential lanes matter most. The designated red zone around Bukchon-ro 11-gil permits tourist visits only from **10am to 5pm**; a fine applies outside those hours. It is a home district, not an early-morning photo shortcut.

Choose **Insadong** if you prefer crafts, tea and a gentler walking surface. It connects naturally toward Ikseon-dong and Jongno 3-ga.

Trying to cover every Bukchon alley and every Insadong shop makes both worse. Pick one as the main afternoon.

## Late afternoon: Ikseon-dong

Ikseon-dong works best as a pause rather than a checklist of famous cafés. Its lanes are small and queues distort the experience. Walk once, choose the place that has room, and leave the most viral doorway to someone else.

## What we would skip

Do not arrive in Bukchon before 10am, do not enter private courtyards, and do not rent a hanbok solely because a guide incorrectly promised free entry without checking current palace policy. Confirm benefits at the palace you intend to visit.

## How to shorten the route

For a half-day, choose one side of Gyeongbokgung. Go west for **Seochon and Tongin Market**, or east for **Insadong and Ikseon-dong**. Bukchon adds hills and visitor restrictions, so it is the first stop to remove when mobility, heat or time is a concern.

## FAQ

### Can tourists visit Bukchon early in the morning?

Not in the designated red zone around Bukchon-ro 11-gil. Tourist visits there are restricted from 5pm to 10am, with enforcement and fines. Other parts of the wider area may have different guidance, so follow signs on site.

### Is Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon and Insadong walkable in one day?

Yes for a comfortable walker, but it is a full day rather than a compact half-day. Expect roughly 6–8 kilometres plus palace grounds and hills. Drop either Bukchon or Seochon when you want a slower pace.

### Should Tongin Market come before or after Bukchon?

Before. Tongin Market and Seochon are west of Gyeongbokgung, while Bukchon, Insadong and Ikseon-dong are east. Visiting Tongin after Ikseon requires unnecessary backtracking.

### Is a hanok the clothing worn at the palace?

No. **Hanbok** means traditional Korean clothing; **hanok** means a traditional Korean house. Rental terms and palace admission benefits should be checked with the palace before visiting.
