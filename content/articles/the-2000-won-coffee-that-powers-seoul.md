Walk through Seoul around 1 p.m. on a weekday and you'll notice something. Office workers heading back from lunch. Students on their way to class. People crossing the street with laptops and tote bags. And in so many of their hands: **a giant plastic cup of iced Americano.**

The cup is big. Almost comically big. The price? **Around ₩2,000.**

Think of ₩2,000 as a ballpark for a basic takeaway Americano: prices vary by chain, size, hot or iced option, and location. This isn't a happy-hour deal or some hidden local bargain.

Walk down an ordinary street in Seoul and you might pass a MEGA MGC COFFEE, then a COMPOSE COFFEE a few steps later, with a Paik's Coffee across the road. Sometimes, they're practically next door to each other. In Seoul, there's a coffee shop next to a coffee shop next to another coffee shop. At lunchtime, they can all have a line.

The ₩2,000 coffee isn't just cheap coffee anymore. **It's part of how Seoul gets through the day.**

## Koreans really, really love coffee

One on the way to work. Another after lunch. Maybe one more when the afternoon slump hits. Even a casual "Want to talk for a bit?" often becomes "Want to grab a coffee?"

Around office districts, you can watch the ritual happen every weekday. Lunch ends, people stream toward nearby cafés, and minutes later they return to their desks carrying enormous iced Americanos.

Coffee in Korea isn't always about *going to a café*. Sometimes, it's simply **part of the rhythm of the day.** And that everyday demand has created a distinctly Korean category: **Budget coffee.** Cheap. Big. Fast. Everywhere.

## How can coffee be this cheap?

Korea's budget coffee chains tend to follow a simple formula: **Low prices + large sizes + fast takeaway + locations everywhere.**

Stores are often small, takeaway volume is high, and drinks are standardized for speed. Instead of making a large margin on every cup, the model is built around selling a lot of them.

For customers, the price also makes ordering a second coffee feel like no big deal. For a visitor, the appeal is easy to understand: **Cheap. Fast. Convenient.** At this price, budget coffee isn't really an occasional café purchase anymore. It's an **everyday drink**.

## Now they're selling more than coffee

There's another interesting shift happening inside Korea's budget coffee shops. **They're becoming places to grab a quick meal, too.**

![A large iced Americano beside a small bowl of tteokbokki on a cafe counter](https://yisdsaetxgrboqlirvgb.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/media/articles/the-2000-won-coffee-that-powers-seoul/coffee-and-a-quick-bite.jpg)

*Coffee and a quick bite: the everyday cafe stop is becoming a small meal, too. AI-generated editorial illustration; not a specific chain’s menu item.*

Café food used to mean cookies, cake or maybe a sandwich. Now, alongside bagels and salt bread, you might find something rather unexpected. **Tteokbokki. Chicken in a cup. Even kimchi fried rice.**

Wait — kimchi fried rice at a coffee shop? Yes.

Mega has expanded into items such as [chicken in a cup](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/business/11986601) and [kimchi fried rice](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/06/23/korean-cafes-reinvent-themselves-as-full-meal-destinations), while Compose has added [tteokbokki](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/business/11986601) to its quick-meal lineup. Menus and availability vary by store and season.

And it makes sense. As eating out in Seoul gets more expensive, a roughly ₩2,000 coffee plus a few-thousand-won snack can become an inexpensive breakfast, light lunch or afternoon meal.

Coffee and bread on the way to work. Coffee and something small instead of a full lunch. An iced Americano and a snack in the afternoon.

What began as a place for **cheap, oversized coffee** is gradually becoming something between a café and a convenience store. A small piece of **everyday urban infrastructure**.

## This isn't Seoul's "best" coffee — and that's the point

None of this means you should come to Seoul and drink nothing but ₩2,000 Americanos. Quite the opposite.

Seoul has an exceptional independent and specialty coffee scene. Neighborhoods like Seongsu, Yeonnam, Hannam and Seochon are filled with cafés roasting their own beans, obsessing over brewing methods and turning coffee into an experience worth traveling across town for.

Budget coffee doesn't replace that culture. **It serves a completely different purpose.**

On the way to work? A ₩2,000 Americano. Business meeting? Maybe Starbucks. Catching up with a friend? A beautiful independent café. Saturday afternoon? Specialty coffee in Seongsu.

Koreans don't necessarily choose between cheap coffee and good coffee. They drink different coffee for different moments.

**One is coffee you seek out.\
The other is coffee you live with.**

Both are part of Seoul.

## And yes, Koreans drink it iced in winter

![A person in a quilted winter coat holding a large iced Americano on a cold street](https://yisdsaetxgrboqlirvgb.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/media/articles/the-2000-won-coffee-that-powers-seoul/iced-americano-in-winter.jpg)

*Puffer coat, scarf, iced Americano. A very Seoul combination. AI-generated editorial illustration.*

You can't talk about Korean coffee culture without talking about the **iced Americano**. In Korean, it's shortened to **"아아" (ah-ah)** — the first syllables of *iced Americano* in Korean.

And yes, people drink it in winter. You can be walking through Seoul in a padded coat and scarf, in below-freezing weather, and still see someone carrying a giant cup filled with ice.

There's even a Korean expression for these people: **얼죽아 (eol-juk-ah).** Short for: **"얼어 죽어도 아이스 아메리카노."**

Or, roughly: **"Iced Americano, even if I freeze to death."**

It sounds like an exaggeration. Spend a few winter days in Seoul and it won't anymore.

## Try it like a local

One day in Seoul, skip the famous café you've saved on Instagram. Just walk into whichever budget coffee shop happens to be in front of you. Mega. Compose. Mammoth. It doesn't really matter.

Order an iced Americano. Maybe pick a snack you've never seen at a coffee shop before. Look at the price, take your enormous cup, and walk through Seoul around lunchtime.

Within a few minutes, you'll probably notice dozens of people carrying a cup that looks remarkably similar to yours. That's a very ordinary afternoon in Seoul.

The ₩2,000 coffee has a particular job. **The coffee you grab on the way to work.** **The second coffee after lunch.** **The giant iced Americano that sits beside your laptop all afternoon.** And increasingly, the place where you can grab a quick bite on a busy day.

In Seoul, coffee doesn't always have to be an occasion. Sometimes, it's simply part of the day.

**And maybe that's why the ₩2,000 coffee keeps Seoul running.**
