Somebody always asks this at a dinner, usually around the second drink. *Okay but which brands. Just tell me which brands.*

The honest problem with answering is that the list of famous Korean brands and the list of good ones overlap without matching. Some names are enormous abroad and unremarkable here. Some are on every second bathroom shelf in Seoul and have no international profile at all.

So this is the list we'd actually send. Five brands, sorted by the job, chosen from what Korea rebuys rather than what travels well on video.

## Quick answer

**Round Lab** for basics. **Torriden** for hydration. **Ma:nyo** for cleansing. **Aestura** when skin is unhappy. **Mediheal** for masks and pads.

That's a complete routine from five brands, all of which have topped their category in Olive Young's awards — a ranking computed from roughly 180 million real purchases rather than from editors.

## 1. Round Lab (라운드랩) — the one we'd hand over first

**What it's for:** the big, cheap, boring, essential things.

Round Lab took two categories in the 2025 awards: the toner category with the 1025 Dokdo Toner, and suncare with the Birch Juice sun cream. Those are the two products in a routine you use most and think about least, and this brand is very good at exactly that.

**Why we'd start here:** nothing about it is clever. The toner is a large bottle of watery hydration that doesn't also try to exfoliate. The sunscreen is a sunscreen you'll actually reapply. Most people's skincare improves more from doing the basics well than from adding an active.

**Start with:** the 1025 Dokdo Toner. Use it with your hands, generously.

## 2. Torriden (토리든) — the hydration one

**What it's for:** dehydration, which is most people, most of the time.

Torriden won **three separate categories** in 2025 — serum, masks, and a lip essence. That's an unusual result. Brands normally win one thing and coast; a three-category sweep in a single year suggests a formulation team that understands texture rather than a marketing team that got lucky.

**Why it's on the list:** the Dive-In serum is the low-molecular hyaluronic acid layer done without tack. If you've ever put on a hydrating serum and then spent an hour feeling faintly sticky, this is the version that solves that.

**Start with:** Dive-In serum. Also good if you're oily — oily skin is very often dehydrated skin that overcompensated.

## 3. Ma:nyo (마녀공장) — the cleansing one

**What it's for:** taking sunscreen off properly, which is the step most people get wrong.

The Pure Cleansing Oil has won the cleansing category **five years running.** Five. In a market that turns over as fast as this one, that's less a trend than a fact.

**Why it matters more than it sounds:** if you're going to wear Korean sunscreen daily — and you should — you need something that removes it completely. A face wash alone doesn't. Congestion that people blame on their moisturizer is very often incomplete cleansing.

**Start with:** the Pure Cleansing Oil. Emulsify it with wet hands before you rinse.

## 4. Aestura (에스트라) — the one for when skin is unhappy

**What it's for:** compromised, reactive, tight, flaky skin. Not glow. Repair.

This is the brand Koreans reach for when something has gone wrong. It's Amorepacific's derma line, with a heritage that traces back to a pharmaceutical division founded in 1982, and Euromonitor ranked it Korea's **number one dermocosmetic skincare brand** by retail sales across all channels in 2025.

The Atobarrier365 Cream has taken the cream category two years running. Amorepacific's own figure is that one sells roughly **every five seconds.**

**Why it's here:** every starter list needs one product that isn't fun. This is it. You will be very glad to own it the week your barrier gives out. [Our barrier repair routine](/articles/korean-barrier-repair-routine) explains when that's happening.

## 5. Mediheal (메디힐) — the volume one

**What it's for:** masks and toner pads, bought by the box.

Mediheal's madecassoside pads took the pad category in 2025, and the sheet masks are the reason a lot of people first heard of Korean skincare at all.

**Why we still recommend it:** this is the cheapest way to change how your face looks in ten minutes, and pads in particular are the most efficient thing to carry home — light, flat, and the sort of product you use up rather than admire. [How we actually use toner pads](/articles/toner-pads-as-mini-masks) has the specifics.

**Buy the box, not the singles.** The per-sheet price difference is not small.

## The ones you already know

You've heard of **Beauty of Joseon**, **COSRX**, **Anua**, **TIRTIR**, and **medicube**. They're real brands making real products, and several of them are genuinely excellent.

They're also global-first operations. Their center of gravity is your feed and your country's Amazon, and most of them are now available at home for close to what you'd pay here once you count the flight. We'd think carefully before spending your Seoul shopping window on any of them.

That's not a knock. It's just the difference between a brand you need to be in Korea for and one you don't. [What we'd actually buy at Olive Young](/articles/what-to-buy-at-olive-young) draws that line product by product.

## If you want one makeup thing

**too cool for school** took the makeup category in 2025 with the Frottage Pencil. If you want a single Korean makeup item rather than a whole base routine, that's the one with a real domestic mandate behind it.

For skin-finish makeup — cushions, tints — the category moves fast enough that we'd rather point you at [what's winning right now](/articles/what-to-buy-at-olive-young) than name something that'll be superseded by the time you land.

## What we'd skip at the start

**Buying five brands at once.** You've just changed climate, water, and sleep schedule. Adding six new products on top means that if something reacts, you'll never know what.

**Actives on week one.** Retinal, high-percentage acids, anything with a warning on the box. Buy them, start them at home on a normal Tuesday.

**Sets and kits.** They're one product you wanted and four you didn't.

**Assuming Korean brands are automatically gentle.** Some of the most-hyped products here are potent, and "Korean skincare is gentle" is a marketing idea, not a formulation rule. Patch test.

## FAQ

**What Korean skincare brand should a beginner start with?**  
Round Lab, for the basics — its toner and sunscreen both won their categories in Olive Young's 2025 awards. Beginners improve faster from doing cleansing, hydration, and sun protection properly than from adding an active.

**Are Korean beauty brands cheaper in Korea?**  
For domestically focused brands, usually yes and often substantially, especially with 1+1 promotions. For the globally distributed names — Beauty of Joseon, COSRX, Anua — the gap is much smaller and sometimes nonexistent.

**Which Korean brand is best for sensitive skin?**  
Aestura is the one most commonly reached for here when skin is genuinely compromised. It's Amorepacific's derma line and Euromonitor's top-ranked dermocosmetic skincare brand in Korea for 2025. That said, "sensitive" covers many different problems — patch test regardless of brand.

**How many Korean skincare products do I actually need?**  
Four: a cleanser, a hydrating layer, a moisturizer, and sunscreen. Everything else is optimization. The ten-step routine is a marketing artifact more than a Korean daily reality.

**Is Olive Young's awards list a good way to choose brands?**  
It's the best popularity signal available, since it's calculated from roughly 180 million purchases rather than editorial picks. Treat it as evidence of what works for a lot of people, not as a match for your specific skin.
