Spoilers for KPop Demon Hunters.

If you watched Rumi and Jinu getting closer and wanted to say, “You two clearly like each other. Just kiss already!”—you’re probably wondering what all that hesitation is about.

Rumi and Jinu never kiss in KPop Demon Hunters. Director Maggie Kang has described the appeal of their restrained romance: “The restraint is sexier.” [1](https://v.daum.net/v/YYMmet6Frs) Korean dating terms such as Gobaek and Sseom offer useful context for their hesitation, though they don’t establish why the filmmakers left out a kiss.

Let’s start with something that can get lost in translation: when does going on dates turn into being a couple?

## What Is Gobaek in Korean Dating?

Gobaek (고백) means a confession of feelings. In Korean dating, it can include asking someone to become a couple. You may have heard it in a K-drama. “Confession” sounds quite grand in English, but the invitation can be simple: shall we be together? If they accept, you have an agreed beginning. [3](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/2865329)

Saying yes still leaves plenty to discover about each other. You’ve agreed to be a couple, and you can keep getting to know each other from there.

In a Korea Herald interview, a Korean man recalled asking his Canadian partner to be exclusive on their second date. She hadn’t expected to make things official so soon. The timing surprised her. [3](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/2865329)

## What Does Sseom Mean?

Sseom (썸) is the in-between stage when two people seem romantically interested but haven’t agreed to become a couple. Picture them messaging each other, finding excuses to meet and wondering whether the feeling is mutual. [4](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00113921251390904)

And yes, the word comes from the English “something.” Think of saying, “There’s something going on between those two.” Korean shortened that something to Sseom—a little word for the feeling that you might be more than friends. [6](https://times.uos.ac.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=1676)

## Is Sseom the Same as a Situationship?

Sseom and Situationship overlap: both can describe a connection that hasn’t become an official relationship. The English term blends “situation” and “relationship” and is now in dictionaries. There’s romance, or sometimes physical intimacy, but the people involved haven’t clearly defined what they are to each other. [6](https://times.uos.ac.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=1676) [7](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/situationship)

The two terms overlap, though they don’t match every time. Sseom often describes the flirting and uncertainty before becoming a couple. A Situationship can also describe an ongoing connection without a clear commitment. Depending on the people, “the talking stage” might fit their Sseom just as well. [4](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00113921251390904) [7](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/situationship)

Imagine asking a friend whether someone is her boyfriend and getting, “Well… we’ve got something going on.” That’s the feeling. A Gobaek can be the conversation that finally clears things up—if they both want to take that next step.

## Why Can a Kiss Feel Like a Bigger Step?

Now imagine that you’re used to going on dates, kissing if there’s chemistry, and deciding later whether you want an exclusive relationship. Being asked to become a couple before much of that happens might feel surprisingly quick.

For someone who expects to agree on the relationship first, the same order may feel reassuring: we’ve said we want to be together, and now we can become more comfortable with each other, including physically.

That expectation came up in The Korea Herald’s coverage of Rumi and Jinu. One Korean interviewee described a familiar sequence of confessing feelings before physical affection, while noting that younger generations follow it less strictly. She connected that expectation to screen romances where a kiss confirms mutual love. [1](https://v.daum.net/v/YYMmet6Frs)

It helps explain how two viewers can want different things from the same scene. One is waiting for the characters to try a kiss and see how it feels. The other is waiting for them to feel sure of each other, so that a kiss means something they both understand.

Of course, real people mix up the order. Someone can read a kiss as “we’re together,” while the other person is still figuring things out. A gesture alone doesn’t settle that question.

And conversations about becoming official happen elsewhere, too. Research on “defining the relationship,” often shortened to DTR, describes young people talking through commitment and clearing up uncertainty. There’s no single timetable followed by everyone in Korea, America or anywhere else. [5](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0265407520918932)

## Back to Rumi and Jinu

For Rumi, letting Jinu get close means letting him see something she has worked hard to hide. That makes their quiet moments especially tender. She’s finding out whether she can be herself with him.

The dating context gives us one way to understand their caution: when closeness feels tied to choosing and trusting someone, a small touch can carry a lot of hope. Their story also comes with secrets, enemies and demons. They have rather more to sort out than an awkward second date.

The film never gives them a kiss, or a neat little scene where they agree to start dating. We’re left enjoying—and perhaps getting a little frustrated by—the moments when they come so close.

## Why Single’s Inferno Feels Familiar

Think about how invested you can become in whether someone sits next to their crush or chooses them at the end of Single’s Inferno. By then, walking away hand in hand can feel like the moment you’ve been waiting for.

If you’re coming from Love Island or Too Hot to Handle, it’s easy to notice a different pace. The show’s producers told Netflix that even recruiting the first cast involved explaining the concept and addressing concerns about comparisons with Too Hot to Handle. The format itself shapes what we see. [2](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/singles-inferno-season-1-reunion-location-questions)

Still, the feeling is familiar: after all that wondering, do these two finally choose each other? That’s part of the fun of watching Rumi and Jinu, too.

Knowing a little about Gobaek and Sseom won’t stop you wishing they’d kiss. But it might help you understand why someone else is already smiling over the way their hands meet.
