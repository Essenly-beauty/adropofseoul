Every visitor gets sent to Seongsu, and they should. But there's a specific kind of afternoon Seongsu can't give you, and Hannam can.

Seongsu is a neighborhood in the middle of becoming something. Hannam already is. It's draped across a hillside above the river, between embassies and old money and the Leeum, and its storefronts have the confidence not to advertise. Nothing here is queuing. Nothing is designed to be photographed from a specific corner.

It's also, unglamorously, **the neighborhood where we send friends who are tired.** Four days into Seoul, when the volume has become a lot, this is where you go.

## Quick answer

**Start at Hangangjin Station**, not Itaewon. Lunch, then the **Leeum Museum of Art**, then the galleries, then the shopping stretch, ending at **Sounds Hannam** around dusk.

Four to five hours. **Wear proper shoes** — this is a hill with staircases, and the maps do not convey that.

**Don't come on a Monday.** The museum and most galleries close.

## First, the geography

Hannam runs roughly along one road between **Itaewon Station and Hangangjin Station**, and the part locals actually mean when they say Hannam is the Hangangjin end.

Between those two stations you'll find the stretch people call **Comme des Garçons street** — named for the flagship that anchored it before the rest arrived. That's the shopping spine.

Everything else is uphill or downhill from it. The neighborhood is genuinely steep in places, with staircases connecting levels, and the walk that looks like four minutes on a map is often four minutes plus a climb. Plan fewer stops than you think.

## 2pm: Leeum

Start here while you still have energy for it.

The **Leeum Museum of Art**, run by the Samsung Foundation of Culture, holds both Korean traditional art and contemporary work, and the buildings themselves are the reason to go even if you're indifferent to the collections. It's three structures by three different architects, which is a strange decision that somehow worked.

**The insider move:** the art shop. It functions as a genuine design store — curated art books, objects, and the kind of thing that makes a much better souvenir than anything with a Seoul skyline printed on it. You can visit it without doing the whole museum.

**Check hours before you go.** Museum schedules and ticketing here change, and Mondays are typically closed.

## 3:30pm: the galleries, and why they're free

This is the part most guides skip and the part we'd insist on.

Over the past several years the international art world quietly chose Hannam as its Seoul address. **Pace Gallery** opened in Korea in 2017 and moved to a larger Hannam space in summer 2021. **Lehmann Maupin** sits right on the road between Itaewon and Hangangjin, which makes it the easiest drop-in of the group. **Thaddaeus Ropac** opened its Seoul branch here. **Foundry Seoul** occupies part of the Gucci Gaok building.

**Why this is worth your time even if you don't care about art:** they're free, they take fifteen minutes each, they're air-conditioned, and nobody will approach you. In a city where most cultural spaces involve a ticket and a queue, walking into a blue-chip gallery, looking at three rooms, and walking out is a genuine luxury.

**Practical:** most close Sundays and Mondays and shut around 6pm. Check current shows before you build the afternoon around them — between exhibitions, a gallery may simply be closed.

## 5pm: the shopping stretch

Hannam's retail is the opposite of a haul. Small footprints, few staff, high specificity.

The reason to shop here is **Korean designer labels with their own flagships** — the independent fashion houses that opened rooms here rather than concessions in a department store. International names have a presence too, but they're not why you came.

**How to shop it:** walk the stretch once without entering anywhere, then go back to the two or three that looked like your kind of thing. The shops are small enough that browsing all of them is both exhausting and slightly awkward.

**On the beauty side, set your expectations correctly.** Hannam is not a beauty shopping neighborhood in the way Seongsu or Myeongdong are — there's no flagship row, no drugstore run. What it has is fragrance and curated small shops, which is a different pleasure entirely. If you want to actually buy Korean beauty, do it elsewhere; [here's where](/articles/where-to-shop-k-beauty-beyond-olive-young).

## 6pm: Sounds Hannam

End here, because it's built for ending.

**Sounds Hannam** is a low-rise complex of shops, cafés, restaurants, a bookstore, and gallery space arranged around courtyards — an urban-resort layout rather than a mall one. Fashion and lifestyle brands from Saint Laurent to Aesop have space in it.

What makes it the right last stop is that it doesn't require a decision. You can get a coffee, sit outside, look through a bookstore, and let the day end without anyone hurrying you.

## Dinner, and an honest note

Hannam eats well and it eats expensively. This is the neighborhood where a good dinner costs what a good dinner costs, and there's no local hack around it.

If that's not the budget, walk fifteen minutes down toward Itaewon proper, where the price ladder is much longer. If it is the budget, book — the good rooms here are small and full on weekends.

## What we'd skip

**Arriving on a Monday.** Museum closed, most galleries closed. You'll have walked up a hill for a shopping street.

**Trying to combine it with Itaewon nightlife.** They're adjacent and they are not the same evening. Hannam at 6pm is a different proposition from Itaewon at 11pm, and doing both means doing neither well.

**Driving or taxiing between stops.** The stretch is genuinely walkable and the traffic on that road is not. Walk it, slowly.

**Treating it as a shopping haul.** You will spend more per item and buy fewer items. That's the trade, and it's the correct one for this neighborhood.

## Why we'd send you here

Most Seoul itineraries are additive — one more neighborhood, one more café, one more thing. Hannam is the one we'd suggest as a subtraction.

There is no landmark you have to see. Nothing closes if you miss it. You walk up a hill, look at some art you didn't plan on, buy one thing, and sit outside until it gets dark. Four days into a Seoul trip, that's usually the afternoon people remember.

## FAQ

**Is Hannam-dong worth visiting?**  
Yes, if you want art, design, and a slower pace rather than shopping volume or nightlife. It's the neighborhood where Seoul's galleries and independent fashion flagships cluster, and it's the best half-day in the city for people who've had enough of crowds.

**How long do you need in Hannam?**  
Four to five hours covers a museum visit, two or three galleries, the shopping stretch, and a coffee. It's a half-day neighborhood rather than a full-day one.

**What's the closest station to Hannam-dong?**  
Hangangjin Station is the one to use — the part locals mean by Hannam is at that end. Itaewon Station works too, and walking between the two covers most of the neighborhood.

**Is Hannam good for K-beauty shopping?**  
Not particularly. It has fragrance and small curated shops rather than flagships or drugstores. For actual beauty shopping, Seongsu and Myeongdong are the neighborhoods; Hannam is for taste rather than volume.

**When are the Hannam galleries open?**  
Typically Tuesday through Saturday, closing around 6pm, with Sundays and Mondays usually closed — and they may shut entirely between exhibitions. Check current shows before planning the afternoon around a specific gallery.
