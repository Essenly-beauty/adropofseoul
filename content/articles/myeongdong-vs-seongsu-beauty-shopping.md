People ask us to settle this like it's a fight. Myeongdong or Seongsu. Pick one.

We can't, really, because they're not answering the same question. Myeongdong is a shopping floor that was built, quite deliberately, for people exactly like you. Seongsu is where the industry that fills that floor actually goes to work. Choosing between them is less about which is *better* and more about which afternoon you want to have.

Here's how we'd explain it to someone with two days and a suitcase.

## Quick answer

**Myeongdong** if you want volume, English-language help, tax refunds, and one dense hour where you buy everything on your list. **Seongsu** if you want to see what Korean beauty looks like right now — flagships, popups, a slower day with coffee in it — and you're not trying to fill a suitcase.

With one full day, do **Seongsu in the morning and Myeongdong after dark**. That order matters, and we'll get to why.

## Myeongdong: honest about what it is

명동, *myeong-dong*. Two subway stops serve it — Myeongdong on Line 4 and Euljiro 1-ga on Line 2 — and between them sit several blocks of cosmetics stores stacked more or less on top of each other.

What it does well is genuinely well. Range is enormous. Staff speak English, Chinese, Japanese, often all three. Tax refund desks are normal here rather than an event. If your goal is to walk in with a list and walk out with the list completed, nowhere in Korea is faster.

What you should know going in: **nobody who lives here shops in Myeongdong for beauty.** Not out of snobbery — it's just that the same chain stores exist near our apartments without the crowd. When we do go, it's for the street food carts in the evening, and even that's a once-a-year thing.

The touting is real. People will hand you flyers and free masks and steer you toward doorways. It is not dangerous or dishonest, it is just relentless, and it wears people down faster than they expect. Two hours is plenty. Four hours is a mistake.

On the street stalls selling masks ten-for-a-thousand-won: fine as souvenirs, unremarkable as skincare. If you want masks worth using, buy a box in a proper store — see [the Olive Young guide](/articles/olive-young-shopping-guide) for what's worth carrying home.

## Seongsu: where it's actually made

성수동, *seong-su-dong*, in Seongdong-gu, on Line 2. Old shoe factories and printing warehouses that turned, over about a decade, into the neighborhood where brands open flagships and run popups to see whether an idea has legs.

You will see it called "the Brooklyn of Seoul" in roughly every article written about it. We'd retire that phrase. What's actually happening is more specific and more interesting: Seongsu is where Korean beauty tests itself in public. A brand takes a warehouse for three weeks, builds something strange in it, and watches how people move through the space. Then it's gone and something else is there.

That's the appeal, and also the catch. **Seongsu is not a shopping district.** You'll find fewer products at worse convenience than one Myeongdong block. What you get instead is the feeling of the industry in motion, good coffee, and a neighborhood people actually spend Saturdays in.

If you go, go with a route rather than a vague plan — the warehouses don't announce themselves. ## Popups, and the queue problem

Seongsu popups are frequently free, frequently beautiful, and frequently have a queue you'll regret joining. Some use a reservation system that assumes a Korean phone number, which quietly excludes most visitors.

Our advice: treat popups as weather. If you walk past one with a short line, go in. If the line curls around the block, that's a signal about social media, not about the experience. Don't build the day around a specific one.

## Doing both in one day

Seongsu first, Myeongdong second, and here's the reasoning.

Seongsu is at its best in daylight, when the warehouses are open and the cafés aren't full. It also asks for energy — walking, deciding, looking. Do it while you still have some.

Myeongdong is at its worst around four in the afternoon and genuinely good after six, when the food carts come out and the shopping becomes something you do while eating a hotteok. Ending there means your heavy bags happen at the end of the day rather than being carried around Seongsu all morning.

The transit is straightforward: Line 2 from Seongsu to Euljiro 1-ga and walk, or continue to Euljiro 3-ga and transfer if you'd rather come up closer to Myeongdong station. Fifteen to twenty-five minutes depending on the change. Check it in [Naver Map](/articles/how-to-use-naver-map) rather than Google, which is unreliable for this in Korea.

## If you only have one afternoon

Pick by what you'll regret missing.

If you came to Korea partly to buy things and you'd be annoyed to fly home without them — Myeongdong. Go at six, give it two hours, be done.

If you have Korean skincare at home already and what you actually want is to understand it — Seongsu. You'll buy less and come back with more.

## FAQ

**Is Myeongdong or Seongsu better for K-beauty shopping?**  
Myeongdong, if shopping is the goal — the range is far bigger and the tax refund and English support are set up for visitors. Seongsu is better for seeing current Korean beauty culture through flagships and popups, but it is not a place to fill a suitcase.

**Do Koreans shop for beauty in Myeongdong?**  
Rarely. The same chain stores are near where we live, without the crowds. Myeongdong is mostly a visitor district now, which is not a criticism — it does that job very well.

**How much time should I give each neighborhood?**  
About two hours for Myeongdong, half a day for Seongsu. Myeongdong is dense and tiring; Seongsu is spread out and rewards wandering.

**Can I do Myeongdong and Seongsu in one day?**  
Yes, and it works well in that order — Seongsu in the morning, Myeongdong after 6pm. They're roughly fifteen to twenty-five minutes apart on Line 2 depending on where you get off.

**Are Seongsu popups worth queueing for?**  
Usually not. Short line, go in; long line, keep walking. Many also use booking systems that need a Korean phone number, so don't plan a day around one.
