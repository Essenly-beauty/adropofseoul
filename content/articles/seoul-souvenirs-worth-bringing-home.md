The souvenir problem is always the same. You want to bring something back. You don't want it to be a fridge magnet with a palace on it. And you have about forty minutes and a suitcase with a specific amount of room left.

Our rule for this list: **would somebody who lives here own it?** Not "is it Korean," which is how you end up with a hanbok-print coaster set. The test is whether the object is in an actual apartment in this city, being used.

Here are ten that pass.

## Quick answer

**The Italy towel. Roasted barley tea. Sesame oil. A heated eyelash curler. Hand cream. Seaweed. Stationery. A perfume you blended yourself.**

Total cost, all eight: less than one duty-free gift set. And every one of them gets used.

## 1. The Italy towel (이태리 타올)

The best souvenir in Korea costs about ₩1,000 and fits in a pocket.

**이태리 타올** — *itaeri taol*, "Italy towel" — is the small, rough, usually green or pink exfoliating mitt used in every Korean bathhouse. The name comes from the Italian viscose fabric it was originally made from; there is nothing else Italian about it.

**Why it's the pick:** it works. Genuinely, slightly alarmingly. It weighs nothing, costs nothing, is available in any supermarket or Daiso, and it's the single most specific object in Korean body care. Also, it's a conversation.

**Use it on damp, soaked skin** — not dry, not soapy — and not more than once a week. [The jjimjilbang guide](/articles/korean-bathhouse-jjimjilbang-guide) covers the full ritual it belongs to.

## 2. Roasted barley tea (보리차)

A bag of roasted barley, boiled into a pot, is what a lot of Koreans drink instead of water. Unsweetened, caffeine-free, warm in winter and cold in summer.

**Why it travels:** it's dry, it's light, it's cheap, and it's the easiest Korean habit to actually keep. [More on that habit here.](/articles/korean-wellness-habits-worth-keeping) Corn silk tea (옥수수수염차) is the sweeter alternative.

**Buy it at:** a regular supermarket, not a souvenir shop. Boxes of teabags are the practical format; loose roasted barley is better if you'll commit.

## 3. Sesame oil (참기름)

A small bottle of properly pressed Korean sesame oil is not the same product as what's on most supermarket shelves abroad, and the difference is not subtle — it's darker, nuttier, and used as a finishing oil rather than a cooking one.

**Buy it at:** a traditional market, where it's often pressed on site, or a decent supermarket. **Wrap it well and check it in.** Glass, liquid, and airport security do not mix.

## 4. Gim (김) — seaweed, the good kind

Not the plain sheets. The seasoned, salted, sesame-oiled snack seaweed that Korean households buy in multipacks.

**Why:** it's flat, it's light, it costs almost nothing, and everyone you give it to will finish it in one sitting and then ask where to buy more. It's the highest-satisfaction-per-gram item in this article.

## 5. A heated eyelash curler

Specifically, the category. **Fillimilli's** heated curler won Olive Young's beauty tools category in the 2025 awards, and it's the item we most often buy as a gift.

**Why it's a great souvenir:** it costs very little, nobody abroad has one, it produces a visible result immediately, and Korean beauty tools are a genuinely under-exported category. [More on the brands nobody has caught onto yet.](/articles/korean-skincare-brands-on-our-radar)

## 6. Hand cream, by the handful

**BEYOND's Classic Hand Cream Deep Moisture** took the hand care category in 2025 and costs under ₩10,000. For gifting purposes it has the best appearance-to-price ratio in any Korean store.

If you want the elevated version, **Granhand** makes hand creams at a friendlier scale than the fragrance houses, and **Tamburins** essentially built its reputation on one. [The fragrance guide](/articles/korean-fragrance-seoul) has the full picture.

**Buy four.** You will be glad on the flight home when someone asks what you brought them.

## 7. A perfume you made

The best souvenir we can suggest isn't bought.

Seoul has a real perfume-workshop culture, and two to three hours produces a bottle nobody else has. [Greedy Scent](/seoul/places/greedy-scent-bukchon) does it inside the hanok village, blending from thirty-plus notes — reviewers keep describing the result the same way, as the memory of the trip in a bottle. [Lumiere](/seoul/places/lumiere-perfume-seongsu) is the one-on-one version; [.NOTE](/seoul/places/note-private-self-perfume-class-seongsu) is self-paced by tablet.

**Book ahead**, and don't wear fragrance that day.

## 8. Stationery

Korea takes paper seriously — notebooks, pens, sticker sheets, tape, the whole desk economy. Daiso alone will absorb an hour and about ₩15,000.

**The upgrade version:** the art shop at the **Leeum Museum** in [Hannam](/articles/hannam-afternoon-local-guide) functions as a proper design store, with curated art books and objects. It's the best place in Seoul to buy something that looks considered.

## 9. Toner pads and sheet masks — by the box

Flat, light, cheap here, expensive abroad. Buy the boxes rather than the singles; the per-unit gap is significant.

[How we actually use toner pads](/articles/toner-pads-as-mini-masks), and [what we'd put in the basket](/articles/what-to-buy-at-olive-young).

## 10. Instant coffee sticks (커피믹스)

We're aware this is unglamorous. It's also what's in every Korean office, every guesthouse, and a great many home cupboards.

**Why it's on the list:** it's the least aspirational item here and one of the most honest. If you spent a week in Seoul, you drank one of these somewhere. It costs nothing and it tastes exactly like the trip.

## What we'd skip

**Ginseng gift boxes.** Heavy, expensive, beautifully packaged, and almost always unopened a year later. If the recipient doesn't already take ginseng, this is a decorative object.

**Anything with a palace, a hanbok, or a Korean flag printed on it.** Nobody here owns these. That's the whole test.

**Kimchi.** Weight, smell, seals, customs rules that vary by country, and a fridge that isn't yours. Buy the sesame oil instead.

**Unofficial K-pop merchandise from street stalls.** Much of it is unlicensed, the quality varies wildly, and official stores exist. If it's for someone who cares, they will know the difference.

**Face masks bought as individual singles from a tourist-strip shop.** This is the classic mistake — twenty different masks from twenty brands, bought one at a time at the worst price in the city.

**Duty free, generally.** For Korean beauty specifically, the assortment skews to legacy brands and gift sets, and in-store promotions often beat it. [More on where to shop.](/articles/where-to-shop-k-beauty-beyond-olive-young)

## Packing notes

- **Liquids over 100ml go in checked bags.** Sesame oil, full-size hair treatments, fragrance bottles.
- **Glass gets wrapped in clothing**, not in a plastic bag, and goes in the middle of the case.
- **Food is usually fine**, but sealed, commercially packaged food is far less trouble than anything from a market stall. Check your own country's rules on meat and fresh produce before you buy anything ambitious.
- **Leave 20% of the case empty on the way out.** Everyone says this. Almost nobody does it.

## FAQ

**What should I buy in Seoul as a souvenir?**  
Things people here actually own: an Italy towel, roasted barley tea, good sesame oil, seasoned seaweed, hand cream, beauty tools, and stationery. All of them are cheap, light, and get used — which is more than can be said for most gift boxes.

**What is an Italy towel?**  
이태리 타올 — the small, rough exfoliating mitt used in Korean bathhouses, named for the Italian viscose it was originally made from. It costs around ₩1,000, works remarkably well on damp skin, and is the most distinctive object in Korean body care.

**Is duty free good for Korean souvenirs?**  
Not especially. For beauty in particular, the range skews to large legacy brands and gift sets, and regular in-store promotions frequently beat duty-free pricing. Buy fragrance and liquor there if you like; buy everything else in the city.

**Can I bring Korean food home?**  
Sealed, commercially packaged food — seaweed, tea, sauces, snacks — is generally straightforward, though you should check your own country's rules on meat and fresh produce. Kimchi is more trouble than it's worth: weight, smell, and seals that fail.

**What's the best cheap souvenir from Korea?**  
The Italy towel, at roughly ₩1,000. Second place goes to seasoned seaweed and a box of barley tea. All three are available in any supermarket and all three get used, which is the only test that matters.
