Daiso is one of the easiest places in Seoul to lose perspective.

Everything costs ₩1,000, ₩3,000 or ₩5,000.

Suddenly the basket is full.

And because each individual decision feels so small, it's easy to leave
with twenty things you never actually wanted.

So this isn't a list of everything that's popular at Daiso.

It isn't a list of everything we saw on TikTok either.

We looked at what has been selling in Korea, what Korean shoppers keep
reviewing and repurchasing, what travelers keep talking about, and ---
for the beauty products --- what the products actually felt like when we
tried them ourselves.

Our conclusion was fairly simple:

**Daiso is best when you treat it as a place to experiment, solve a
small problem, or pick up something fun without spending much.**

You do not need to rebuild your skincare routine here.

You do not need every viral product.

And cheap alone is not a reason to give something space in your
suitcase.

Our rule for this list:

**Cheap isn't enough. It has to be worth the suitcase space.**

## A quick note on Daiso Beauty

We tried the beauty products on this list ourselves.

The most honest verdict?

**Most of them were... fine. And at Daiso prices, that's kind of the
point.**

We didn't discover a ₩5,000 serum that transformed our skin overnight.
We also weren't expecting to.

If your skin is already in good condition and you aren't particularly
sensitive, many of these products may simply feel like perfectly decent
skincare.

That is not necessarily a criticism.

For ₩1,000 to ₩5,000, Daiso Beauty is a low-commitment way to try an
ingredient, play with a format you normally wouldn't buy, or bring home
a few small products for family and friends.

The category where we came away more impressed was **sheet masks**.

K-beauty sheet masks are already such a mature, competitive category
that even inexpensive products can feel genuinely good --- not merely
"good for the price."

So our beauty recommendations below are less about miracle results and
more about:

**Would we happily use it again? Is it fun to try? And does the value
actually make sense?**

### ADoS TIP

**Can't find the product? Show a staff member the product photo or item

## What we'd skip

The easiest mistake at Daiso is buying something simply because the
downside feels small.

But cheap clutter is still clutter.

We'd generally skip:

-   large or heavy objects without a specific use
-   generic stationery you could buy anywhere
-   duplicates of beauty products you haven't tried
-   fragile souvenirs in large quantities
-   travel accessories you don't actually need
-   ten versions of something just because each one costs ₩1,000

Before something goes into the basket, ask:

**Would I still want this if it weren't this cheap?**

And for travelers, one more:

**Will I actually use this when I get home?**

That usually clears the basket surprisingly quickly.

## Our Daiso rule

Daiso is fun precisely because the stakes are low.

**Low-cost discovery.
