Most beauty itineraries fail on timing rather than on choices.

People do Myeongdong at four in the afternoon, when it's at its worst. They carry a day's shopping around Seongsu's warehouse streets from ten in the morning. They queue for a café at noon that had no queue at ten.

The stops in this day are not surprising. The order is the point. [We've argued elsewhere](/articles/myeongdong-vs-seongsu-beauty-shopping) that Myeongdong and Seongsu aren't competing for the same afternoon — this is what it looks like when you give each of them the hours it's actually good in.

## Quick answer

**Seongsu 10am–4:30pm. Myeongdong 5pm–late.**

Flagships before the queues, a park in the middle, the heavy shopping last so you carry bags for one hour rather than seven.

**Not a Sunday.** Seongsu on a Sunday afternoon is a different, worse experience.

## Before you go

**Naver Map, not Google.** Google still doesn't do reliable walking directions here; [the full explanation is here](/articles/how-to-use-naver-map). Save your stops the night before.

**Bring your passport.** Tax refunds need the physical one.

**Empty bag, comfortable shoes.** You'll walk somewhere between eight and twelve kilometres.

## 10:00 — Coffee in Seongsu, before it fills

Start at **[Onion Seongsu](/seoul/places/onion-seongsu)**, the converted factory widely credited with turning this neighborhood into a café district. The bakery counter is genuinely good rather than merely photogenic.

**Go before 11am.** This is not a soft suggestion. The queue after that is real, and the same room at 10 is a completely different place.

## 10:45 — The flagship row

Now walk. Seongsu's beauty flagships sit within a few minutes of each other, and this stretch is the reason the day exists.

**[Amore Seongsu](/seoul/places/amore-seongsu)** first — Amorepacific's flagship in a converted auto repair shop, more than 2,000 products across 30-plus brands, built as a showroom rather than a store. No checkout pressure. **It has free luggage lockers**, which is the practical detail that changes your day if you're between hotels.

Then, in whatever order the street gives you:

- **[TIRTIR](/seoul/places/tirtir-seongsu)** — two floors built around cushion foundation shade matching, in five zones called Shade Valley. The one genuinely useful thing you can't do online.
- **[Tamburins](/seoul/places/tamburins-seongsu)** — raw concrete, rotating art installations, fragrance. Go for the room as much as the products.
- **[Nonfiction](/seoul/places/nonfiction-seongsu)** — gallery-like, open counters, built for unhurried testing. [More on Korean fragrance here.](/articles/korean-fragrance-seoul)
- **[Amuse](/seoul/places/amuse-seongsu)** — the all-pink Pink House.
- **[fwee](/seoul/places/fwee-seongsu)** — dozens of lip shades laid out for open swatching.

**The rule for this stretch: test, don't buy.** Photograph what you like. Prices here are not better than a drugstore's, and every gram you pick up now you carry for six hours. [Why these rooms are built this way.](/articles/what-makes-a-brand-feel-seoul)

## 12:30 — Lunch, without a reservation

Eat in Seongsu. The neighborhood's food scene grew up alongside its industry and it's genuinely good; our [beauty-and-bites walk](/articles/seongsu-beauty-and-bites) covers the tables in detail.

What we'd avoid is booking. You will be running behind — everyone always is — and a reservation turns the morning into a countdown.

## 13:30 — One more room, your choice

Pick one, not three:

**[Olive Young N Seongsu](/seoul/places/olive-young-n-seongsu)** — the five-floor innovation flagship, each floor organised around a different way of shopping. Worth seeing as a format even if you'll buy in Myeongdong later.

**[LCDC Seoul](/seoul/places/lcdc-seoul)** — four floors, each with its own identity, stacking independent Korean labels. Work upward.

**[EQL Grove](/seoul/places/eql-grove-seongsu)** — a select shop with rotating collaborations with local brands.

## 14:30 — The reset

This is the step people cut, and cutting it is why the evening goes badly.

Get a coffee at **[Mesh Coffee](/seoul/places/mesh-coffee)** — takeout only, no seating, entirely focused on the brew — and walk into **Seoul Forest** with it. Sit down for half an hour. Do nothing.

Four hours of retail decisions is genuinely tiring, and the difference between a good Myeongdong evening and a miserable one is whether you arrive with anything left.

## 15:30 — Optional: book something instead

If you'd rather have a treatment than a second shopping block, this is the slot for it. Seongsu has [head spas and salons](/seoul/neighborhoods/seongsu) — [YOU Hair Salon](/seoul/places/you-hair-salon-seongsu) runs a fifteen-step head spa with English service, and [Mariem](/seoul/places/mariem-hair-head-spa) is the fuller course.

Book it weeks ahead, and if you do, treat it as the end of the day rather than the middle. [What actually happens at a head spa.](/articles/korean-head-spa-first-timer-guide)

## 16:30 — Cross the city

**Line 2 from Seongsu to Euljiro 1-ga**, then walk. Fifteen to twenty-five minutes depending on whether you change at Euljiro 3-ga to come up closer to Myeongdong station.

**Leave at 4:30, not 3:30.** Myeongdong is at its worst around four in the afternoon, when tour groups and people finishing work arrive at the same time and the aisles stop being aisles. Arriving at five is a materially better experience than arriving at four.

## 17:00 — Myeongdong, the actual shopping

Now you buy. Everything you photographed this morning, plus the bulk items.

**Do one full lap of a large Olive Young before picking anything up.** The real discount is the **1+1** tag, not the shelf price, and the serum you grabbed in the first aisle is frequently sitting under a 1+1 tag by the registers. [The full mechanics — branches, promotions, tax refund.](/articles/olive-young-shopping-guide)

**What goes in the basket:** sunscreen (several), cleansers, toner pads, masks by the box, and one thing for whatever your skin is doing this month. [Our named picks.](/articles/what-to-buy-at-olive-young)

**Say the tax refund before they start scanning.** 택스 리펀 돼요? — *taekseu ripeon dwaeyo?* [More phrases.](/articles/korean-phrases-for-beauty-shopping)

**Then walk two minutes to a Daiso** for tools, clips, cotton pads, and containers. Same objects, a fraction of the price. There is one near every Olive Young and nobody tells visitors.

## 18:30 — Eat standing up

The food carts come out in the evening and this is when Myeongdong is genuinely good — shopping becomes something you do while eating a hotteok rather than a task.

## 19:30 — Dinner, or a treatment

Myeongdong is Seoul's densest cluster of visitor-friendly [spas and facial studios](/seoul/neighborhoods/myeongdong), and several run late. Ending a shopping day horizontal is a defensible life choice.

Otherwise: dinner, and don't book that either.

## If you only have half a day

**Do Seongsu, 10am to 2pm.** Skip Myeongdong entirely and buy online when you get home — most of what you'd have bought ships internationally now.

The flagships are the part you can't replicate. A drugstore run is a drugstore run.

## What we'd skip

**Starting in Myeongdong.** You'll carry bags through Seongsu all afternoon and arrive at the flagships with no hands free.

**Sunday.** Seongsu on a Sunday afternoon is queues in every direction. [Here's what a Sunday is actually good for.](/articles/slow-sunday-in-seoul)

**Buying at the flagships.** Test there, buy in Myeongdong, where promotions apply.

**Booking a service in the middle of the day.** It fragments both halves. Put it at the end or on another day.

**Six flagships when you wanted four.** Nobody has ever regretted skipping the fifth concept store. Several people have regretted the walk.

## The swap: Gangnam instead of Myeongdong

If your priority is a service rather than shopping — personal color, a proper salon appointment, a clinic — swap the evening half for [a beauty lover's day in Gangnam](/articles/gangnam-beauty-day) on its own day.

Gangnam doesn't work as a half-day add-on. Everything there needs a reservation, and reservations don't bend around a morning that ran long.

## FAQ

**How many days do I need for K-beauty shopping in Seoul?**  
One well-timed day covers the flagships and a full drugstore run. A second day is worth it only if you want services — personal color, a salon, a head spa — which need their own schedule and advance booking.

**Should I start in Seongsu or Myeongdong?**  
Seongsu, in the morning. Its flagships are best before the queues, and the neighborhood asks for energy and free hands. Myeongdong is better after six and is where the heavy buying should happen, so your bags exist for one hour rather than seven.

**What time is Myeongdong worst?**  
Around four in the afternoon, when tour groups and people finishing work arrive together. Arriving at five is noticeably better, and the street food carts in the evening are part of the appeal.

**Do I need to book anything for this itinerary?**  
No, and that's deliberate — the shopping half works better unbooked. The only exception is a head spa or salon, which needs weeks of notice and is best placed at the end of the day or on a separate one.

**Is one day enough to see Seongsu properly?**  
For the beauty flagships, yes — they're clustered within a short walk. Seongsu also has a full café and design scene that easily fills a second day if you want it.
