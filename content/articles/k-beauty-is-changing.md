If you learned about Korean beauty between roughly 2014 and 2018, you learned a story with three parts: a ten-step routine, a sheet mask, and a snail.

It was a good story. It was mostly accurate at the time. And almost nothing about it describes what's happening here now.

We started this magazine because the gap between those two things has become absurd — and because the most interesting version of Korean beauty is the one that has no international press office.

## Quick answer

**In 2025, Korea passed the United States to become the world's second-largest cosmetics exporter**, at $11.4 billion. Only France is bigger.

But the number that actually matters is this one: **small and mid-size companies accounted for about 71% of those exports.** Not Amorepacific. Not LG. Brands with a handful of staff.

The K-beauty that's winning is not the K-beauty anyone abroad wrote about.

## The four numbers

**$11.4 billion in exports, up 11.8% year on year** — a record, and enough to move Korea past the US into second place globally. France remains first at $24.3 billion; the US fell to third at $10.8 billion. Korea was third itself the year before.

**A trade surplus over $10 billion for the first time.** Cosmetics is now a genuine net-export industry for this country, not a cultural side effect of pop music.

**202 importing countries, up from 172.** In one year. That's not a market expanding; that's a market saturating.

**The United States became the largest destination for the first time**, at roughly $2.2 billion and up 15%. For a decade, the China question was the only question in Korean beauty. It isn't anymore.

## What actually changed: who makes it

Here's the part that reframes everything.

Korean beauty runs on an unusually powerful contract-manufacturing layer. **Cosmax** did about ₩1.6 trillion in revenue last year; **Kolmar Korea** about ₩1.3 trillion; **Cosmecca Korea** about ₩353 billion. Most people reading this have never heard of any of them, and there's a reasonable chance something in your bathroom was made by one.

What that infrastructure does is collapse the cost of starting a brand. A team of three with a point of view can develop a competitive formula, put it in an existing mold, and be selling in months — without a factory, a chemist, or a lab.

That's why the export share held by small companies went from around 55% five years ago to **71.2% in the first half of last year.** The barrier to entry didn't shrink. It essentially disappeared.

**The consequence is that Korean beauty stopped being top-down.** For most of its history it was a conglomerate industry with a heritage-brand structure. Now it's closer to an open platform, and the interesting things are happening at the edges — which is exactly where nobody's international marketing budget reaches.

## What that means when you're standing in a shop

Three practical things follow from it.

**The best-seller list is no longer the big-brand list.** Around 75% of the first-place categories in Olive Young's 2025 awards went to small and mid-size companies. [The winners are mostly names you don't know](/articles/what-to-buy-at-olive-young), and that's a change in kind, not degree.

**Fame abroad and quality here have decoupled.** A brand can be enormous on your feed and unremarkable in a Seoul bathroom, and vice versa. That gap is [the whole premise of our radar series](/articles/korean-skincare-brands-on-our-radar).

**The category boundaries dissolved.** Hair, scalp, supplements, sleep, food. [Skincare formats spread into haircare](/articles/korean-haircare-where-to-start) — scalp ampoules grew about four times faster than shampoo last year — and skincare vocabulary spread into makeup. "Korean beauty" now means something much wider than a face routine.

## The honest part

We're not going to pretend all of this is good.

**Velocity has a cost.** When anyone can launch in months on shared manufacturing, a lot of brands are a marketing layer over a formula somebody else developed. The packaging is the product. Distinguishing genuine formulation work from a good font is now a real skill, and most content in this space isn't even trying.

**Claims outrun evidence.** Exosomes, PDRN, stem cells, slow aging — the vocabulary here moves faster than the data behind it, and it moves fastest in exactly the categories that sell best.

**The standards are heavy.** A culture this fluent about skin is also a culture that scrutinizes it relentlessly, and we'd rather export the [vocabulary](/articles/korean-skin-texture-meaning) than the pressure.

**And the ten-step thing never was what you were told.** Most people here use four or five products. [Skip-care](/articles/korean-skip-care-explained) — deliberately using fewer — has been the actual domestic direction for years.

## What we're doing about it

This magazine is not trying to index Korean beauty. There is too much of it, most of it is uninteresting, and comprehensive coverage is how you end up with a list of 200 serums and no opinion.

We're doing the other thing: **telling you what's worth knowing.**

That means writing about scalp care and fragrance and bathhouses alongside serums, because that's the actual shape of the category now. It means naming what we'd skip as clearly as what we'd buy. It means [sending you to the neighborhoods](/articles/seoul-neighborhoods-guide) rather than the landmarks, and telling you when a globally famous product is cheaper on your own Amazon than in Myeongdong.

And it means one test on every piece before it goes up: **would we actually send this to a friend?**

Not "would this rank." Not "is this comprehensive." Would we, personally, text this to someone we like who is coming to Seoul.

If the answer is no, it doesn't go up.

## FAQ

**Is Korea the biggest cosmetics exporter?**  
Second. Korean cosmetics exports hit a record $11.4 billion in 2025, passing the United States ($10.8 billion) for the first time. France remains first at $24.3 billion.

**Is K-beauty still just about 10-step routines?**  
No, and it largely never was domestically. Most people here use four or five products, and skip-care — deliberately reducing steps — has been the local direction for years. The category has also expanded well past skincare into scalp care, fragrance, supplements, and sleep.

**Who actually drives Korean beauty exports now?**  
Small and mid-size companies, which accounted for roughly 71% of cosmetics exports in the first half of last year, up from about 55% five years ago. The conglomerates are no longer the story.

**Why did Korean indie beauty brands grow so fast?**  
A powerful contract-manufacturing layer — Cosmax, Kolmar Korea, Cosmecca and others — lets a small team develop and launch a competitive product in months without owning a factory or a lab. That collapsed the cost of starting a brand.

**Is China still Korea's biggest beauty market?**  
Not as of 2025. The United States became the largest destination for Korean cosmetics for the first time, at roughly $2.2 billion, and the number of importing countries rose from 172 to 202 in a single year.
